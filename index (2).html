❓ FAQ

Do I need to do the topics in order?

Mostly yes. Fundamentals and Prompt Engineering are the base everything builds on. Handoff, Feedback Loops, and Orchestration assume you already understand how a single agent behaves and how to evaluate it. The colored dots show the rough difficulty climb.

How do I track what I've studied?

Open a topic and hit "Mark as studied" at the bottom — the topic turns green and the overall and per-level progress update. Use the filters (To study / New / by level) to find what is left, and "Continue where I left off" to jump to the next unstudied topic.

What does the NEW badge mean?

It flags anything added since your last visit — a new topic, a new concept, or a new resource. It's worked out automatically per person from what you saw before, so you never need to be told what changed. Opening the topic clears its NEW marks, or use "Mark all as seen" to clear them all at once.

If the page is updated, do I lose my progress?

No. Your checkmarks live in your browser and are keyed to each item's stable ID, not its position — so the author can add, reorder, or expand content and republish freely without touching what you've saved. Only clearing your browser data or hitting "Reset progress" wipes it. (After an update you may need a hard refresh — Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+R — to see the newest version; that only affects what you see, not your saved progress.)

Can I study on more than one device?

Yes — your progress lives in each browser, so open "Sync progress," copy your code on one device, and paste it on another to carry your studied topics across. No account needed.

Can I share this, and do I need an API?

Share the link freely — each visitor keeps their own progress on their own device, no account or server. The concepts apply to any LLM-based agent; to try things hands-on, any chat model with an API works, or mock the responses to practice the architecture.

📖 Glossary

Token — the unit a model reads and writes in; text is split into tokens.
Context window — the finite amount of tokens a model can consider at once.
Embedding — a vector representation of text used to compare meaning.
Vector search — finding the closest chunks to a query by semantic similarity.
RAG — retrieval-augmented generation: fetch relevant context, then answer from it.
Chunking — splitting documents into pieces the retriever can index and find.
Hallucination — confident output that is not grounded in fact.
Temperature — how random a model's sampling is; low is more deterministic.
Few-shot — giving examples in the prompt to shape format and behavior.
Chain-of-thought — asking the model to reason step by step before answering.
Function calling — letting a model invoke structured tools or APIs.
Agent loop — observe, decide, act, repeat — what makes an agent an agent.
LLM-as-judge — using a model to score outputs against a rubric.
Eval — a repeatable test of output quality you run on every change.
RLHF — reinforcement learning from human feedback to shape behavior.
Prompt injection — hidden instructions in input that try to override the system prompt.
Guardrails — checks that keep an agent safe, on-topic, and within limits.
p95 / p99 — tail latency: the slowest 5% / 1% of requests, which averages hide.
